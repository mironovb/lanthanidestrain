**Subject:** Lanthanide extraction: 3D structure adds to the best 2D model

Dear Dr. Vogiatzis,

A first summary of the lanthanide extraction work, with the main result and the
two questions I would like your view on.

We predict log D for Ln(III) extraction complexes, but overall accuracy is not
the useful target. What matters is separation between neighbouring lanthanides,
where the ionic radii differ by about 0.013 Å. So I score the R² for the
predicted difference in log D between two adjacent lanthanides measured with the
same extractant under the same conditions. Zero means no better than assuming
every adjacent pair separates by the average amount. The dataset is 4,746
measurements from SAFE over 162 extractants, 14 lanthanides and 110 papers, with
one Architector / GFN2-xTB complex per (metal, ligand, inner-sphere anion).
Cross-validation is leave-extractants-out, so a ligand is never in both train and
test.

The result is that a neural encoder over the 3D complex supplies
adjacent-lanthanide information that fingerprint models do not have. Adding it to
the strongest single model without 3D input, the fingerprint network, is worth
+0.035 R² [+0.017, +0.065]. The combination anyone would deploy, gradient
boosting plus that network plus the 3D encoder, reaches 0.267 against 0.226
without the 3D arm (figure 1).

The control is what makes that a structural result rather than an ensembling one.
A second model often helps simply because its errors differ, so I built a matched
model with the same architecture, objective, folds and seeds and only the 3D
encoder removed, and put it in the same slot. It is worth +0.006 [-0.017, +0.018],
which is nothing. Against that control directly the 3D arm is +0.030 (figure 2).
Every contrast and its decision rule was committed before the numbers existed,
and the bootstrap resamples whole extractants, since rows on one ligand are not
independent.

Three limits belong with that sentence rather than in an appendix. The effect is
not topological: I built the complexes as Vietoris-Rips filtrations, but deleting
the triangles costs nothing and a plain distance network with no simplices earns
the same slot, so what helps is a learned 3D representation, not the complex.
The size depends on how strictly identical conditions are defined; under the
strictest blocking the estimate is +0.018 and no longer clear of zero, positive
but half as large. Both are in figure 3. And I cannot say what fraction of the
attainable signal this is, because the noise floor of the metric is not
identifiable from this data.

Two questions:

1. Is the CN 9 / CN 8 break at Gd/Tb the right prior for extraction complexes? It
   is applied uniformly, so if it is wrong for a ligand family, that family is
   wrong systematically and the structure QC will not catch it.

2. Does complementarity read as a result to you? What I can defend is that 3D
   structure carries neighbouring-lanthanide information the fingerprints lack,
   worth about +0.035 R² in the best combination. What I cannot defend is that 3D
   beats 2D, because on its own it does not.

Three figures attached. The full write-up is ready whenever you want it,
including the negative results and the audit trail for every dropped row and
rejected structure.

Best,
Bogdan
