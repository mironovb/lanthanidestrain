**Subject:** Lanthanide extraction: first results, and how the data and structures were built

Dear Dr. Vogiatzis,

First write-up of the lanthanide extraction work. I've included how the dataset
and the structures were made, since I expect the questions are there rather than
in the model.

We predict log D for Ln(III) complexes, but overall accuracy isn't the useful
target; separation between neighbouring lanthanides is. So I score the R² for the
predicted difference in log D between two adjacent lanthanides measured under the
same conditions with the same extractant. Zero means no better than assuming every
pair separates by the average amount. Cross-validation is leave-extractants-out,
5 folds by 3 repeats, so a ligand is never in both train and test.

## The data

All of it from SAFE: 31 per-metal exports, 48,138 rows. Lanthanide rows leave
27,168; a finite positive D leaves 26,653; 18,763 exact duplicate records and 225
unparseable SMILES leave 7,665; 1,673 content duplicates leave 5,992.
Deduplication uses 25 scientific fields, with DOI, curator, date and row id kept
out of the key, so one experiment reported in two papers collapses to one row.
Dropped rows keep their reason and surviving rows keep their SAFE id, so anything
here traces back to a paper. After the structure filter below: 4,746 measurements,
162 extractants, 14 lanthanides, 110 papers.

Two condition fields aren't features. SAFE has no pH column, which I think is
deliberate: acidity here is acid identity plus molarity, and 69% of the rows are
nitric acid at 0.1 M or above, where pH is neither measured nor meaningful in the
usual sense. It does cost
us in the roughly 240 rows run in citric, lactic, malonic or tartaric media, where
papers do report pH. The phase ratio is in the export but stored as a
volType/volValue pair my column matching didn't look for. That one is my bug,
though every lanthanide row with a ratio records 1.0, so nothing downstream
changes.

## The structures

Geometry is fixed by chemistry, not by the experiment, so the key is (metal,
ligand, inner-sphere anion) and 5,992 measurements become 1,256 complexes.
Coordination number comes from the metal via the contraction, 9 for La through Gd
and 8 for Tb through Lu. Donors come from the ligand graph, with amide nitrogen
excluded since its lone pair is delocalised into the C=O. Denticity is the largest
chelate pocket, stoichiometry follows by mass balance, and nitrate media above
0.05 M get inner-sphere bidentate nitrate while everything else gets water. That
gives 753 complexes at CN 9 and 503 at CN 8, 876 nitrate and 380 water.
Architector assembles each one with the donors named explicitly and relaxes it
with GFN2-xTB, one conformer, gas phase. 1,234 of the 1,256 built.

I keep a structure only if its longest first-shell bond is under 2.95 Å with a
0.10 Å gap to the next donor, so the shell is unambiguous. 956 pass, which costs
1,246 rows, 21% of the chemically valid data. I think that's the right trade, but
it is a selection on structures and I'd say so in a paper.

## The result

| model | adjacent-lanthanide separation R² |
|---|---|
| CatBoost + fingerprint net + simplicial net | 0.267 |
| CatBoost + fingerprint net | 0.226 |
| same, with a matched 2D model in the 3D slot | 0.221 |
| fingerprint net alone | 0.221 |
| CatBoost alone | 0.142 |

The third row is the one that matters. A third model often helps just because its
errors differ, so I built a control with the same architecture, objective, folds
and seeds, minus the 3D encoder, and put it in the same slot. Adding the 3D model
to the best 2D-only combination is worth +0.038 [0.019, 0.050]; against the
control, +0.045 [0.030, 0.054]. The bootstrap resamples whole extractants, since
rows on one ligand aren't independent, and I committed the test and its decision
rule before computing anything.

Rebuilding at 3.0 and 4.0 Å instead of 3.5 gives +0.038 and +0.033, on complexes
with about half the triangles and twice as many, so 3.5 Å isn't a tuned parameter.

One surprise: the preprocessing applied a quantile transform, which preserves
order and destroys spacing, and a separation factor is spacing. Trees don't care,
which is why it went unnoticed. Fixing that line took the fingerprint network from
about 0.005 to 0.221, and everything above is measured against the corrected
baseline.

## Limits and questions

The structures are single conformers and gas phase, the weakest part of the setup.
CatBoost on 2D descriptors is still better for overall log D, 0.499 against 0.437,
so the 3D arm buys selectivity rather than accuracy.

Three things I'd like your view on:

1. Is the CN 9 / CN 8 aqua-ion break the right prior for extraction complexes? It
   is applied uniformly, so if it's wrong for a ligand family, that family is
   wrong systematically and the QC gate won't catch it.
2. Should the 2.95 Å threshold scale with ionic radius rather than being absolute?
   The contraction spans about 0.18 Å across the series, so a fixed cutoff is
   stricter for Lu than for La and may be discarding one end preferentially.
3. Does complementarity rather than superiority read as a result to you? What I
   can defend is that a 3D topological representation carries
   neighbouring-lanthanide information the fingerprints don't, worth about
   +0.04 R² in the best combination.

Unless you'd rather something else, next I'd build a plain distance-based 3D
network with no simplicial structure, to see whether "simplicial" or just "3D
message passing" is doing the work.

Five figures attached. The repository has the negative results in one document,
and the audit trail for every dropped row and rejected structure.
