**Subject:** Lanthanide extraction: what the 3D model does and does not buy

Dear Dr. Vogiatzis,

This is the first proper write-up of the lanthanide extraction work. I have put
the dataset and structure construction ahead of the model, because I think the
questions you will want to ask are there.

The target is log D for Ln(III) extraction complexes, but overall accuracy is
not the useful quantity. What matters industrially is separation between
neighbouring lanthanides, where the ionic radii differ by about 0.013 Å. So the
score I report is the R² for the predicted difference in log D between two
adjacent lanthanides measured with the same extractant under the same
conditions. Zero means no better than assuming every adjacent pair separates by
the average amount. Cross-validation is leave-extractants-out, 5 folds by 3
repeats, so a ligand never appears in both train and test.

## The data

All of it from SAFE: 31 per-metal exports, 48,138 rows. Restricting to
lanthanides leaves 27,168; a finite positive D leaves 26,653; removing 18,763
exact duplicate records and 225 unparseable SMILES leaves 7,665; 1,673 content
duplicates leave 5,992. The deduplication key uses 25 scientific fields and
deliberately excludes DOI, curator, date and row id, so one experiment reported
in two papers collapses to a single row. Every dropped row keeps its reason and
every surviving row keeps its SAFE id. After the structure filter below: 4,746
measurements, 162 extractants, 14 lanthanides, 110 papers.

Two condition fields are not features. SAFE has no pH column, which I suspect is
deliberate, since acidity here is acid identity plus molarity and 69% of rows are
nitric acid at 0.1 M or above. It costs us on the roughly 240 rows run in citric,
lactic, malonic or tartaric media, where papers do report pH. The phase ratio is
in the export but stored as a volType/volValue pair my column matching missed.
That one is my bug, though every lanthanide row carrying a ratio records 1.0, so
nothing downstream moves.

## The structures

Geometry is set by chemistry rather than by the experiment, so the key is (metal,
ligand, inner-sphere anion), and 5,992 measurements become 1,256 complexes.
Coordination number follows the contraction, 9 for La through Gd and 8 for Tb
through Lu. Donors come from the ligand graph, with amide nitrogen excluded
because its lone pair is delocalised into the C=O. Denticity is the largest
chelate pocket, stoichiometry follows by mass balance, and nitrate media above
0.05 M get inner-sphere bidentate nitrate while everything else gets water.
Architector assembles each complex with the donors named explicitly and relaxes
it with GFN2-xTB, one conformer, gas phase. 1,234 of 1,256 built. I keep a
structure only if its longest first-shell bond is under 2.95 Å with a 0.10 Å gap
to the next donor, so the shell is unambiguous. 956 pass, which costs 1,246 rows,
21% of the chemically valid data. I think that is the right trade, but it is a
selection on structures and I would say so in a paper.

## The result

Three contrasts, all committed with their decision rules before anything was
computed. The bootstrap resamples whole extractants, since rows on one ligand are
not independent.

| contrast | Δ adjacent-pair R² | 90% CI |
|---|---|---|
| add the 3D model to the best model without it | +0.035 | [+0.017, +0.065] |
| add a matched model with no 3D input instead | +0.006 | [−0.017, +0.018] |
| 3D model against that matched control, same slot | +0.030 | [+0.005, +0.065] |

The middle row is the one I care about. A second model often helps a combination
simply because its errors differ, so I built a control with the same
architecture, objective, folds and seeds and only the 3D encoder removed, and put
it in the same slot. It buys nothing. In the deployable three-model combination
the same comparison runs 0.226 without 3D to 0.267 with it, and 0.221 when the
slot is filled by the control.

One preprocessing finding is worth a sentence on its own, since it applies well
beyond this project. The pipeline applied a quantile transform, which preserves
order and destroys spacing, and a separation factor is spacing. Trees are immune,
which is why nobody caught it. Fixing that one line took the fingerprint network
from about 0.005 to 0.221, and every number above is measured against the
corrected baseline, not the broken one.

## Three things that have to travel with that

First, it is not topological. I built the complexes as Vietoris-Rips filtrations
and message-passed over them, and for a while I thought the simplicial structure
was the point. It is not. Deleting the triangles costs nothing, and a plain
distance network with no simplices at all earns the same slot (+0.028). The
comparison between them is +0.009 with an interval spanning zero in both
directions. The defensible claim is that a learned 3D representation of the
complex carries adjacent-pair information the fingerprints lack. Whether it is
built topologically is not what matters.

Second, it depends on how strictly "same conditions" is defined. The metric
blocks rows into cells, and the published key bins the condition columns, so two
rows can share a cell while differing in extractant concentration. Under a strict
key, where every numeric condition matches and the only thing varying inside a
cell is the lanthanide, the effect is +0.018 [−0.002, +0.037], about half the
size and no longer clear of zero. It is a weakening rather than a reversal; the
estimate stays positive and P(Δ>0) is 0.93. Neither key is clean. The binned one
rewards the model for predicting condition effects; the strict one cuts 552
cells into 2,109 and leaves every cell mean noisier. I report both columns
everywhere now, and I think a paper has to as well.

Third, I cannot tell you what fraction of the attainable signal this is. I tried
three times to estimate the noise floor of the metric and all three fail for the
same reason: 70% of the pairs the metric scores come from cells with no
replicate, and the cells that do carry replicates scatter 26 times more than
those pairs imply. Duplicates appear preferentially where sources disagree.
That is answerable with a designed replicate study and not with this dataset.

## What did not work

Roughly fifteen configurations across two pre-registered campaigns are null or
negative against the same anchor: angular and polyhedral descriptors by three
routes, auxiliary targets on xTB energetics, readout and optimiser changes, a
parametrised pairwise head, and FiLM conditioning that lets the diluent and acid
reach the structural embedding. The pattern is consistent: information that
varies within a composition block does not reach this metric and often hurts it.

I also closed the conformer question rather than leaving it as future work. Using
xtb metadynamics on 120 complexes across 9 whole ligand families, 82% of the
shipped geometries are not the global minimum, and the arbitrariness of which
minimum was found accounts for 69% of the within-family energy scatter. Removing
all of it still leaves a signal-to-noise ratio of 0.32 for the lanthanide trend
in the xTB energetics. So a full CREST campaign, succeeding completely at what it
is for, would not rescue those features. That is also evidence against the
obvious expensive next step of running DFT on single conformers, since the
problem is the conformer rather than the Hamiltonian.

## What I would like your view on

1. Is the CN 9 / CN 8 break at Gd/Tb the right prior for extraction complexes? It
   is applied uniformly, so if it is wrong for a ligand family, that family is
   wrong systematically and the QC gate will not catch it.

2. Should the 2.95 Å first-shell threshold scale with ionic radius instead of
   being absolute? The contraction spans about 0.18 Å across the series, so a
   fixed cutoff is stricter for Lu than for La and may be discarding one end
   preferentially.

3. Does complementarity read as a result to you? What I can defend is that a
   learned 3D representation carries neighbouring-lanthanide information the
   fingerprints do not, worth about +0.035 R² in the best combination, under the
   binned key, and about half that and not significant under the strict one. What
   I cannot defend is that 3D beats 2D, because alone it does not.

4. The dataset is 97.5% neutral solvating extractants, mostly diglycolamides and
   BTBPs. That blocks the cation-exchange versus solvation comparison entirely.
   If there is a source of HDEHP, HEH[EHP] or Cyanex 272 data you would trust,
   that is the single addition that would open the most.

The repository has every pre-registration, the negative results in one document,
and an audit trail for each dropped row and rejected structure. Happy to send
figures or walk through any of it.
